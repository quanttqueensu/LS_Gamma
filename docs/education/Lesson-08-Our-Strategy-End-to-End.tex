\textbf{Goal:} know every rule our desk trades on and how a day runs, from data download to hedge.

\textbf{You need:} Lessons 1--7.

\section{The idea in one line}

Every day, forecast SPY's volatility to expiry. If options are \textbf{too cheap}, buy one and scalp it (long gamma). If they are \textbf{too expensive}, sell one and scalp it (short gamma). Otherwise \textbf{do nothing}.

\section{Rule 1: the signal}

\[\text{VRP} = IV_{ATM} - RV_{forecast}\]

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{VRP} & \textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Trade} \\
\midrule
above +0.02 & \textbf{short} gamma & Sell 1 ATM put, delta hedge \\
below \ensuremath{-}0.05 & \textbf{long} gamma & Buy 1 ATM call, delta hedge \\
in between & \textbf{flat} & No new trade \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\textbf{Why the band is lopsided:} the VRP means IV is usually above RV (Lesson 6). Buying volatility fights that premium, so we demand a bigger edge before buying (IV at least 5 vol points \emph{below} our forecast) than before selling (IV at least 2 points \emph{above} it). The \textbf{flat zone} in between keeps us out when the forecast's edge is too small to beat costs and forecast error.

\section{Rule 2: which option}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Expiry:} the Friday expiry closest to \textbf{30 days}, within 21--45 DTE. One-to-two month options give the best delta-hedged Sharpe ratio (Sepp 2013), and very short-dated gamma swings too fast for once-a-day hedging.

\item \textbf{Strike:} the one closest to SPY's price (\textbf{ATM}), where gamma, vega and the VRP are largest.

\item \textbf{Calls for long gamma, puts for short gamma.} Once hedged, they're nearly the same bet (Lesson 4), but buying calls avoids paying the put skew, and selling puts collects it.

\end{itemize}

\section{Rule 3: size}

\textbf{1 contract per trade, at most 1 open position.} Paper trading is first a test of the machinery. We size up only once we have a P\&L history to measure risk with.

\section{Rule 4: hedging}

\textbf{Once a day}, trade SPY shares to bring the total delta back to zero (Lesson 5).

\section{Rule 5: exits (the first trigger to fire wins)}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{l l >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\toprule
\textbf{Order} & \textbf{Trigger} & \textbf{Rule} \\
\midrule
1 & \textbf{time} & 7 or fewer days to expiry: avoid the last week's gamma and theta cliff \\
2 & \textbf{stop} & Short put: loss \ensuremath{\geq} 1\ensuremath{\times} premium received. Long call: loss \ensuremath{\geq} 50\% of premium paid. Measured on option P\&L \textbf{plus} hedge P\&L \\
3 & \textbf{profit} & Short put: profit \ensuremath{\geq} 50\% of premium received \\
4 & \textbf{flip} & The signal turns to the opposite side: close, then open the other side \\
5 & \textbf{flat} & The signal has been flat for 3 days in a row \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Every number above lives in \textbf{\texttt{configs/\allowbreak{}paper.\allowbreak{}toml}}, so changing a rule never needs a code change.

\section{A trading day, step by step (about 10:15 ET)}

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Data:} the pipeline downloads SPY prices and VIX indexes (yfinance) plus the SPY option chain (IBKR, delayed quotes).

\item \textbf{Forecast:} fit GARCH on SPY returns and forecast volatility to the target expiry.

\item \textbf{Signal:} compare the forecast with that expiry's ATM IV, then log it.

\item \textbf{Exits:} if a position is open, mark it at the mid, compute option + hedge P\&L, and check the five triggers.

\item \textbf{Entry:} if no position is open and the signal isn't flat, open the chosen option at mid + \$0.05.

\item \textbf{Hedge:} trade shares to make delta zero.

\item \textbf{Log:} everything goes to \texttt{results/\allowbreak{}paper/\allowbreak{}} (\texttt{signals}, \texttt{trades}, \texttt{hedges}, \texttt{position.\allowbreak{}json}).

\end{enumerate}

Run it with \texttt{python -\allowbreak{}m lsgamma.\allowbreak{}trading.\allowbreak{}runner} (add \texttt{-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}dry-\allowbreak{}run} to see decisions without trading). All commands are in \texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}scripts.\allowbreak{}md}.

\textbf{Design rule:} the algos only \emph{describe} trades. Every decision lives in \texttt{signals/\allowbreak{}} and \texttt{trading/\allowbreak{}}. That's why new signals or hedgers (e.g. an RL hedger) can be swapped in without touching execution.

\section{Safety rules}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Paper only.} \texttt{broker.\allowbreak{}connect()} refuses any account that isn't a paper account (IDs starting with ``DU'').

\item \textbf{Never commit data or keys.} Market data from the club's R2 bucket and WRDS is licensed to Queen's. \texttt{.\allowbreak{}env}, \texttt{data/\allowbreak{}} and \texttt{results/\allowbreak{}} are never committed.

\end{itemize}

\section{Words to know}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Signal band:} the thresholds that turn the VRP into long, short or flat.

\item \textbf{Exit trigger:} a rule that closes a position.

\item \textbf{Dry run:} a run that decides but doesn't trade.

\item \textbf{Registry:} a dictionary mapping names to interchangeable models.

\end{itemize}
